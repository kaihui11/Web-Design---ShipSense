What it does

Six things your desk does every day, in one place

ShipSense is an internal tool, not a rate marketplace. Every screen exists because someone quoting freight needed it.

Machine-learned rate forecast

An XGBoost model trained on six years of lane history, crude oil and USD/EUR movements projects the daily rate per 40ft container for the next 90 business days.

The cheapest window nearby

Give it an intended ship date and it scans the surrounding days for the lowest forecast rate, so a shipment that can flex by three days isn't booked on the worst one.

Quotations that stay put

Issuing a quotation freezes the price, the reference and the forecast run behind it. A later model run can never quietly move a number a client was already given.

Executive dashboard

Forecast KPIs, a trend chart, a calendar laid out on real weekdays, and the historical actuals behind it — for the people who need the shape, not the single number.

Market impact scenarios

An exchange-rate slider against the 90-day path, clearly labelled as an illustration. It is never used to price a quote — the model produces one central path, not a rate surface.

A record of every decision

Requests, revisions, confirmations and cancellations are kept. A confirmed or cancelled quote can't be edited away, because it is the record of something that actually happened.

How it works

From enquiry to issued quotation in three steps

  1. 1

    Enter the shipment

    Company, person in charge, container count and the customer's intended ship date. That's the whole form — the lane is fixed for this deployment.

  2. 2

    Read the forecast

    The rate for the requested date, the days either side of it, and the cheapest of them highlighted, along with how the latest model run revised its own earlier view.

  3. 3

    Issue the quotation

    Apply the standard markup, generate a referenced quotation with a validity window, and record what the client decided against it.

The lane

One route, forecast properly

This deployment forecasts a single trade lane: Rotterdam to New York, priced in US dollars per 40ft container. A model that covers one lane well is worth more to a quoting desk than one that covers forty badly, so the scope is deliberate rather than a limitation waiting to be fixed.

The underlying index is the Xeneta XSI-C reading for the North Europe to US East Coast trade, with crude oil and the USD/EUR rate carried as explanatory features.
