What it does

Six things your desk does every day, in one place

ShipSense is an internal tool, not a rate marketplace. Every screen exists because someone quoting freight needed it.

Machine-learned rate forecast

An XGBoost model trained on six years of lane history, crude oil and USD/EUR movements projects the daily rate per 40ft container for the next 90 business days.

The cheapest window nearby

Give it an intended ship date and it scans the surrounding days for the lowest forecast rate, so a shipment that can flex by three days isn't booked on the worst one.

Quotations that stay put

Issuing a quotation freezes the price, the reference and the forecast run behind it. A later model run can never quietly move a number a client was already given.

Executive dashboard

Forecast KPIs, a trend chart, a calendar laid out on real weekdays, and the historical actuals behind it — for the people who need the shape, not the single number.

Market impact scenarios

An exchange-rate slider against the 90-day path, clearly labelled as an illustration. It is never used to price a quote — the model produces one central path, not a rate surface.

A record of every decision

Requests, revisions, confirmations and cancellations are kept. A confirmed or cancelled quote can't be edited away, because it is the record of something that actually happened.

How it works

From enquiry to issued quotation in three steps

  1. 1

    Enter the shipment

    Company, person in charge, container count and the customer's intended ship date, on the New Forecast page. That's the whole form — the lane is fixed for this deployment.

  2. 2

    Read the forecast

    The rate for the requested date, the days either side of it, and the cheapest of them highlighted, along with how the latest model run revised its own earlier view.

  3. 3

    Issue the quotation

    Apply the standard markup, generate a referenced quotation with a validity window, and record what the client decided against it.

The lane

One route, forecast properly

This deployment forecasts a single trade lane: Rotterdam to New York, priced in US dollars per 40ft container. A model that covers one lane well is worth more to a quoting desk than one that covers forty badly, so the scope is deliberate rather than a limitation waiting to be fixed.

The underlying index is the Xeneta XSI-C reading for the North Europe to US East Coast trade, with crude oil and the USD/EUR rate carried as explanatory features.

Built by a freight desk, for a freight desk

ShipSense exists because quoting ocean freight from a rate sheet that was already three weeks old was costing us money in both directions — margin when the market fell, business when it rose.

The company

Good Fortune Logistics Sdn Bhd

We have been moving containers for customers out of Kuala Lumpur since 2009 — a freight forwarder, not a carrier. Our people spend their day answering one question on behalf of exporters: what will it cost to put this box on a ship, and when should it sail?

For most of that time the answer came from a spreadsheet of contracted rates, refreshed monthly, adjusted by whoever happened to have read the market that week. It worked. It was also consistently wrong in the same expensive way: a rate sheet is a photograph of a market that has already moved on, and the gap between the sheet and reality is absorbed either by our margin or by the customer's trust.

ShipSense replaces the photograph with a projection. It is an internal tool: there is no public rate lookup, no self-service booking, and no account anyone outside the company can open. Everything it produces goes through a person on our desk.

The model

What is actually under the forecast

No black box for its own sake. If a number on the screen cannot be explained to a customer, it should not be on the screen.

The data

Daily readings of the Xeneta XSI-C index for the North Europe to US East Coast trade, going back to 2018, alongside crude oil prices, the USD/EUR rate, and GDP and inflation for both ends of the lane.

The method

Gradient-boosted regression trees (XGBoost) over rolling time-series features — recent levels, momentum and seasonality — producing one central rate path per business day for the next 90 days.

The check

Scored on a validation split and then on a hold-out period the model never saw during training. Both sets of figures are published below rather than only the flattering one.

Honest limits

What ShipSense does not claim

A forecasting tool earns its keep by being trusted, and nothing costs trust faster than a number that quietly meant less than it looked like it meant.

It is not a rate guarantee

The forecast is a central estimate, not a contracted price. What binds us is the quotation a person on our desk issues from it, with its own reference and validity window.

It does not cover every lane

One trade lane is modelled. Quotes on any other routing are produced the old way and are not touched by this system.

The scenario slider is an illustration

The Market Impact page shows how the path might respond to a different exchange rate. The pipeline produces one central path and cannot truly answer that question, so nothing is ever priced from it — and the page says so.

It cannot see a shock coming

Canal closures, strikes and sudden capacity withdrawal are not predictable from price history. The model catches drift and seasonality well; it will be caught out by an event, and our validity windows are short for that reason.

How we build it

Four rules the system is held to

01 A quoted price never moves

Once a quotation is issued, its price, reference and the forecast run behind it are frozen in the database, not merely in the interface. Re-quoting creates a new record; it never rewrites the old one.

02 A decision is permanent

When a client confirms or cancels, that record stops being editable. Business history is not something the interface should let anyone tidy up.

03 Rules live in the database

Every rule the screens enforce is enforced again in Postgres. Disabled buttons stop mistakes; constraints and triggers stop everything else.

04 Uncertainty is labelled

Where a figure is illustrative, held constant, or older than it looks, the screen says so in the place the figure appears — not in a footnote nobody opens.

Support and the freight desk

Answers to the questions this workspace raises most often, and a way through to a person for everything else. A human reads every message — usually within one business day.

Help

Getting unstuck

Most support requests are one of these five. Each answer says where in the workspace to go, not just what the rule is.

The Intended Ship Date I need is rejected

The date picker on New Forecast only accepts dates the current model run actually covers, and the hint under the field prints that range. A date outside it has no forecast behind it, so the system will not invent one. Wait for the next run, or quote that shipment the old way.

I can't edit or delete a record

Only records that are still Pending and have no quotation issued against them can be changed or removed from Forecast History. Once a quotation is issued, or the client has confirmed or cancelled, the record is the evidence of something that happened and the database refuses to alter it. Re-quoting creates a new record instead.

The price changed between my forecast and my quotation

A quotation carries the standard 20% markup and a 48-hour validity window, both applied at the moment it is issued. The underlying forecast can be revised by a later model run, but an issued quotation keeps the run it was priced from — that run's date is printed on the record.

A page says there is no data

The dashboard and the forecast pages read the latest model run directly. If no run has been ingested yet, or the database is unreachable, the pages say so rather than showing a stale number. Tell us via the form below and include which page and roughly when you saw it.

Can I price a quote from the Market Impact slider?

No. The model produces one central rate path, not a rate surface, so the exchange-rate scenario on Market Impact is an illustration of direction and rough scale only. Quotes are always priced from the forecast itself.
